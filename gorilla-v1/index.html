Thermal Energy in Motion

Bioluminescence — nature's own thermal display

Living Light · Living Heat

Jellyfish use bioluminescence — converting chemical energy directly into cold light. Unlike thermal cameras that detect infrared heat emission, bioluminescence is light produced at ambient body temperature through luciferase enzyme reactions. This is why jellyfish glow cyan-blue: they emit light at 480nm, the precise wavelength of their internal chemical reaction.

In thermal imaging, cold-blooded marine animals like jellyfish appear nearly invisible — their bodies match the surrounding ocean temperature. Only their metabolically active organs — the bell muscles and gonads — show as faint warm signatures above the ambient water temperature.

BODY TEMP: ~15°C · OCEAN: 12°C · GLOW: 480nm CYAN

Why thermal imaging uses hot colors

Thermal cameras detect infrared radiation — electromagnetic waves just below visible red at 700nm–1mm wavelengths. Scientists needed a visual language for invisible data, so they mapped it to colors our brains already use emotionally: red for hot, blue for cold.

This alignment between human psychology and physics is profound. Our brains evolved to associate red-orange with fire and danger. Thermal cameras made that intuition scientifically precise.

  • 700nm+ · Deep Red · Near Infrared · Body heat signatures
  • 780nm–3μm · Short-wave IR · Flames, heated metal
  • 3–8μm · Mid-wave IR · Industrial heat sources
  • 8–14μm · Long-wave IR · Room temperature objects

Why cold maps to blue and violet

Blue and violet represent minimal heat radiation. This aligns perfectly with human psychology: blue is universally associated with cold water, ice, night — all low-energy environments. The FLIR Rainbow palette runs from deep violet (coldest) through blue, cyan, green, yellow, orange to white (hottest).

  • Near zero · −40°C surface · Minimal emission · Deep violet
  • 0–10°C · Frost, ice surfaces · Low radiation · Dark blue
  • 10–20°C · Cool ambient · Moderate radiation · Cyan
  • 20–30°C · Room temperature · Cyan-green transition

The Two Thermal Hemispheres

Heat and Cold — why they work differently in design

01
HOT BAND · 620nm → 750nm+

Red · Orange
Pink · Flame

Hot colors are high-energy electromagnetic waves. Physical reality maps directly to human psychology — our brains are wired to treat red-orange as urgent and activating.

  • Red (700nm) activates the amygdala in under 80ms — faster than any other color. Universal for stop, danger, and emergency across every culture.
  • Orange (620nm) stimulates L and M cones simultaneously — the brain reads this as food and energy. Every major fast food chain uses this scientifically documented response.
  • Yellow (580nm) sits at the photopic sensitivity peak — the most detectable wavelength in daylight. Emergency vests and school buses are yellow by scientific mandate.

02
COLD BAND · 380nm → 500nm

Blue · Cyan
Violet · Frost

Cold colors are lower-energy waves. Less energy per photon — and this registers in human cognition as calming, safe, and trustworthy. Blue is the world's most universally liked color.

  • Blue (450nm) activates ipRGCs — a third photoreceptor type connected directly to the circadian clock. It measurably lowers blood pressure and heart rate.
  • Cyan (495nm) has the highest legibility in high-glare environments — dominant in airport signage and emergency wayfinding worldwide.
  • Violet (400nm) carries maximum visible photon energy. In thermal maps: the coldest zones near absolute zero emission.

Color Science · Visual Psychology

Why each color does what it does to your brain

🔴

Red — Maximum Urgency

700nm. Photons trigger L-cone cells most aggressively. The brain processes red before any other color — it literally arrives first. Used in warnings and emergencies because the neural response is biological, not learned.

700nm · L-CONE DOMINANT · RESPONSE <80ms · AMYGDALA ACTIVATION
🟠

Orange — Energy & Appetite

620nm. Activates both L and M cones simultaneously — dual stimulation the brain reads as high-energy and food-associated. Rare in nature outside ripe fruit. Every major fast food chain exploits this measured effect.

620nm · L+M DUAL CONE · APPETITE +15% · FAST FOOD STANDARD
🟡

Yellow — First to Fire

580nm. The peak of the human photopic sensitivity curve — the most detectable wavelength in daylight. Your eye is physically optimized for this frequency. Emergency vehicles and safety equipment are yellow because physics demands it.

580nm · PHOTOPIC PEAK · FASTEST DAYLIGHT DETECTION
🟢

Green — Center & Balance

530nm. Mathematical center of the visible spectrum. Stimulates M-cones at peak response — humans have more M-cones than L or S-cones, so green is what we're optimized for. Universal "go" and "safe" signal across all cultures.

530nm · M-CONE PEAK · MOST ABUNDANT CONE TYPE · UNIVERSAL SAFE
🔵

Blue — Trust & Calm

450nm. Activates S-cones AND intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (ipRGCs) that regulate the circadian rhythm. Measurably lowers cortisol, blood pressure, and heart rate. The world's most universally liked color.

450nm · S-CONE + ipRGC · BLOOD PRESSURE −5 TO −7mmHg
🟣

Violet — Edge of Sight

400nm — the very boundary of human visual perception. Highest energy of visible light. Because violet is rare in nature, the brain assigns it to elevated abstract concepts: creativity, royalty, and mystery.

380–400nm · S-CONE · HIGHEST VISIBLE PHOTON ENERGY
