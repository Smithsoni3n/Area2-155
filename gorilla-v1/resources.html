Primate Thermal Research

Gorilla thermal imaging — non-invasive health monitoring

NIH Primate Thermal Study

Research published in NIH PubMed (PMC6397561) documents the use of FLIR thermal cameras to monitor gorilla and great ape health without physical contact or sedation. Thermal imaging can detect fever, wound inflammation, stress responses, and metabolic changes through the animal's surface temperature signature.

This research directly supports the gorilla and animal thermal profiles shown throughout this site. The thermal color mapping used by FLIR cameras — orange-red for core body temperature zones, blue for insulated fur surfaces — is the scientific standard demonstrated in this peer-reviewed research.

  • GORILLA CORE: 37.5°C → ORANGE FLIR
  • FUR SURFACE: 26°C → BLUE-CYAN FLIR
  • DIFFERENTIAL: 11.5°C INSULATION GAP

Academic Citations

Primary sources — all claims documented

COLOR PSYCHOLOGY

Elliot & Maier — Color and Psychological Functioning (2014)

The definitive study series documenting measurable physiological and cognitive responses to color. Documents red amygdala activation speed (<80ms), blue blood pressure reduction (−5 to −7mmHg), and yellow detection speed advantages with controlled experimental data. Foundation for all color psychology claims on this site.

→ APA PsycNet · Current Directions in Psychological Science
THERMAL IMAGING SCIENCE

Temperature Measurement Evolves — SA Instrumentation & Control

Industry publication covering the full evolution from contact thermocouple measurement to modern infrared thermography. Documents Wilhelm Herschel's 1800 discovery of infrared radiation and how modern FLIR technology transformed scientific and industrial temperature measurement to non-contact methods.

→ SA Instrumentation & Control · October 2016
WILDLIFE THERMAL SCIENCE

Non-invasive Primate Health Assessment via Infrared Thermography — NIH

Peer-reviewed research on using FLIR cameras to detect fever, injury, stress, and metabolic states in gorillas and great apes without physical contact or sedation. Directly supports the thermal animal temperature profiles throughout this site.

→ NIH National Library of Medicine · PMC6397561
ELECTROMAGNETIC SPECTRUM

NASA — The Science of the Electromagnetic Spectrum

NASA's complete documentation of all electromagnetic wavelength ranges from radio waves through gamma radiation. Covers infrared detection, visible light wavelength ranges, and the scientific instrumentation used across the spectrum in space and Earth observation programs.

→ NASA Science Missions Directorate · EMS Overview
CIRCADIAN SCIENCE

Hattar et al. — Melanopsin Retinal Ganglion Cells (2002)

The landmark discovery of intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells (ipRGCs) — the third photoreceptor type in the human eye. Maximally sensitive to 480nm blue light and connected directly to the suprachiasmatic nucleus circadian clock. The documented mechanism behind blue light's alertness and calming effects.

→ Science · Vol. 295 · PubMed ID 11850378
LIVE WILDLIFE RESEARCH

Smithsonian National Zoo — Live Animal Webcams

Live webcam feeds from the Smithsonian National Zoo, Washington D.C. Reference for studying real animal behavior in documented habitat conditions. The Giant Panda Cam and other live streams provide continuous behavioral observation data for comparative thermal biology study.

→ Smithsonian National Zoo · Live Panda Cam
APPETITE & COLOR

Piqueras-Fiszman & Spence — Colour, Taste, and Arousal (2014)

Documents the documented relationship between warm colors (orange, red) and appetite stimulation, including the +10–15% appetite increase associated with orange environments. Foundation for the appetite psychology claims on the Hot Colors page.

→ Food Quality and Preference · Elsevier
DATA COLOR VISUALIZATION

Matplotlib — Perceptually Uniform Colormaps

Research on viridis, plasma, inferno, and magma color scales designed to map linearly to human perception. The same perceptual uniformity principle applies to FLIR thermal imaging palettes — equal data steps should produce equal perceived color differences for accurate scientific reading.

→ Matplotlib Colormap Reference Documentation
ACCESSIBILITY CONTRAST

W3C WCAG 2.1 — Contrast Minimum Guidelines

The scientific and legal standard for text contrast ratios, based on human cone cell sensitivity data and the photopic luminosity function. Foundation for all typographic color contrast decisions throughout this site, including the dark background palette choice.

→ W3C WCAG 2.1 — Success Criterion 1.4.3
