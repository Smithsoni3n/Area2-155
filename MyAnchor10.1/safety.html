Service Dog Etiquette

Safety Tips

Learn how to interact respectfully & safely with service dogs & their handlers. Your awareness can save lives.

Respect Their Focus

Service dogs are working. Petting or making eye contact can distract them from their job & put their handler at risk.

Always ask the handler first & respect if they say no. A distracted service dog cannot do their life-saving work.

Avoid Distractions

Click to learn more

Avoid Distractions

Whistling, barking, or making noises to get a service dog's attention is dangerous & disrespectful.

These sounds can break concentration during critical moments when the dog is alerting to a medical condition or keeping the handler safe.

Food Safety

Food Safety

Never offer food to a service dog. Many have dietary restrictions & treats can make them sick or interfere with training.

Food can also distract them from detecting medical alerts that could save their handler's life.

Privacy Respect

Privacy Respect

Taking photos or videos without permission invades privacy. Many handlers prefer not to be photographed.

Always ask first & accept "no" gracefully. The handler's comfort & safety come first.

Keep Distance

Click to learn more

Keep Distance

Keep your pets away from service dogs. Even friendly dogs can distract or frighten a working dog.

A traumatized service dog may not be able to continue working. Cross the street or create space when you see a service dog team.

Respect Privacy

Click to learn more

Respect Privacy

Don't interrogate handlers about their disability. Many disabilities are invisible, including PTSD, anxiety & seizure disorders.

Businesses may only legally ask two questions: "Is this a service dog?" & "What tasks is it trained to perform?"

Remember

Service dogs are medical equipment, not entertainment. Every distraction could mean a missed alert, a panic attack, a seizure, or worse.

When you see a service dog, the best thing you can do is ignore them & let them work. Their handler's life may depend on it.
