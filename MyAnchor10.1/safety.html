Respect & Understanding

Service Dog Safety & Etiquette

Understanding how to interact with service dogs keeps both the dog and handler safe. These guidelines help create a respectful environment for working teams.

Don't Pet

Service dogs are working when they're with their handler. Petting them can distract them from critical tasks like alerting to medical emergencies or providing balance support.

💡 Always ask permission first, and don't be offended if the answer is no.

Don't Feed

Service dogs follow strict diets and feeding schedules. Giving them food can cause health issues, digestive problems, or break their focus during critical moments.

💡 Even a small treat can disrupt months of training.

Don't Distract

Making noises, whistling, or calling to a service dog pulls their attention away from their handler. This distraction could prevent them from detecting an oncoming medical episode.

💡 Pretend the dog is invisible—admire from afar silently.

Talk to the Handler

If you have questions or concerns, speak to the handler, not the dog. The handler can explain what their dog does and whether interaction is appropriate at that moment.

💡 Handlers appreciate respectful curiosity.

Give Space

Service dog teams need personal space to work effectively. Crowding or blocking their path can create dangerous situations, especially in emergencies.

💡 Maintain at least 6 feet of distance when possible.

Know the Law

Under the ADA, service dogs are allowed in all public spaces. Asking someone to remove their service dog or denying access is illegal and harmful.

💡 Only two questions are legally allowed: "Is this a service dog?" and "What tasks is it trained to perform?"

Why This Matters

Service dogs aren't pets—they're medical equipment with four legs and a heartbeat. When someone distracts or interferes with a service dog, they're potentially putting a person's life at risk.

For me, Dude's alerts have prevented dangerous situations. When he's distracted, those seconds matter. Respecting service dog teams means respecting our safety, independence, and dignity.

How to Help

The best way to support service dog teams? Give space, stay quiet around working dogs, and educate others. If you see someone violating these guidelines, politely intervene or notify staff.

Share this information. Advocate for access rights. And remember: every time you respect a service dog team's space, you're helping someone live their life more fully.
