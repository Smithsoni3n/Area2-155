Understanding Service Dogs

Service Dog Facts

Learn about the incredible work service dogs do & how they transform lives every single day.

Extensive Training

2+ Years of Training

Service dogs undergo rigorous training for 2-3 years, learning over 90 commands & tasks specific to their handler's disability.

They master public access skills, task work & maintaining focus in any environment.

Training never stops—it's a lifelong commitment.

PTSD Support

Trained PTSD Tasks

PTSD service dogs perform critical tasks: grounding during flashbacks, creating physical barriers in crowds, interrupting nightmares & providing deep pressure therapy.

They can detect physical signs of anxiety before the handler even notices.

Legal Protection

Click to learn more

ADA Rights

Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, service dogs are allowed in ALL public spaces, including restaurants, stores, hospitals & transportation.

Businesses can only ask two questions: Is this a service dog? What tasks is it trained to perform?

No certification or registration is legally required.

Not Pets

Click to learn more

Medical Equipment

Service dogs are working medical equipment, not pets. They provide life-saving assistance & must remain focused on their handler at all times.

Distracting a service dog can be dangerous & may prevent them from performing critical tasks.

PTSD is Real

Not all disabilities are visible. PTSD, anxiety disorders & other psychiatric conditions are just as valid & debilitating as physical disabilities.

Service dogs for psychiatric conditions perform measurable, trained tasks that mitigate disability symptoms.

Never assume someone "doesn't look disabled enough" for a service dog.

Handler's Rights

Click to learn more

Privacy Matters

Handlers are NOT required to disclose their disability, provide documentation, or explain what their dog does for them beyond the two ADA questions.

Respect their privacy & their right to access public spaces safely.

Did You Know?

Service dogs can detect changes in heart rate, blood pressure & cortisol levels through scent. They often alert to panic attacks, dissociative episodes, or flashbacks before the handler consciously realizes what's happening.

This early intervention can prevent full-blown episodes & help handlers use coping strategies before symptoms escalate.
