HOT SPECTRUM DOSSIER

Investigating red, orange, and pink as high-energy visual signals in cognition, marketing, and affective physiology.

Red Psychology

Red heightens arousal and avoidance vigilance in evaluative contexts; Elliot & Maier's review (2014) links red to danger, dominance, and performance pressure effects across cultures.

Citation: Elliot, A. J., & Maier, M. A. (2014). Color Psychology. Annual Review of Psychology.

Orange Psychology

Orange blends red urgency with yellow optimism, often generating perceptions of warmth, affordability, and sociability; applied studies in retail show increased browsing under warm ambient palettes.

Reference set includes environmental color and consumer response work synthesized in color-marketing literature.

Pink Psychology

Chellappa et al. (2011) found evening short-wavelength light alters alertness and endocrine timing; pink-adjacent spectra in digital contexts are often used to signal emotional intensity and novelty.

Citation: Chellappa, S. L., et al. (2011). Non-visual effects of light on melatonin, alertness and cognitive performance.
