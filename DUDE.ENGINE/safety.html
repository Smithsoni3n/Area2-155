Safety & Etiquette

"Distraction is Dangerous." The Rules of Engagement.

Respect the Vest

Medical Guardian: The vest signals this dog is on duty, safeguarding his handler’s health. He’s not a pet in the moment — he’s a trained medical partner.

Not for Play: Treating him as “friendly” undermines his role. He’s trained to stay alert, not to socialize.

Protection First: His focus is on detecting and responding to changes in the handler’s body. Every distraction risks breaking that protective shield.

Do Not Touch

Breaks Protective Focus: Touching him shifts his attention away from guarding the handler. Even a brief pat can interrupt his ability to detect medical changes.

Not Affection, Interference: What feels like kindness to you is actually interference with his protective duty.

Think Security: Just as you wouldn’t tamper with a security guard’s equipment, don’t interfere with a service dog’s work.

Do Not Talk

Confuses Command Structure: Service dogs are trained to respond only to their handler’s cues. Outside voices create confusion and weaken his protective role.

Instinct vs. Duty: Calling to him forces a choice between instinct (responding to you) and responsibility (protecting the handler). That hesitation can matter.

Respect the Silence: Silence allows him to remain locked on his job, guarding, monitoring, and protecting.
