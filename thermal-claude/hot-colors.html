RED

Red triggers the fastest autonomic nervous system response of any color. Research by Elliot et al. (2007) demonstrated that red exposure measurably increases heart rate, blood pressure, and respiratory rate within seconds — an evolved response to fire, blood, and predator signals.

In thermal imaging, deep red represents temperatures above 50°C — the surface temperature of actively contracting muscle tissue, inflamed tissue, or the metabolic core of a large predator at peak activity.

PSYCHOLOGY

Threat Detection Bias

The human visual cortex has evolved preferential neural pathways for detecting red among natural foliage — a primate survival adaptation for spotting ripe fruit and predator eyes.

PHYSIOLOGY

Cortisol Elevation

Sustained red light exposure elevates cortisol and testosterone in controlled studies. Athletes in red environments show measurably higher grip strength and explosive output (Hill & Barton, 2005).

THERMAL MAPPING

50–70°C Signature

In FLIR thermal cameras calibrated for biology, deep red-white zones indicate surfaces exceeding 50°C. In wildlife monitoring, this marks the face, core, and active muscles of large mammals.

ORANGE

Orange occupies the psychological intersection of red's urgency and yellow's optimism. Studies in hospitality design show orange environments increase social interaction frequency by up to 23% compared to neutral spaces (Jalil et al., 2012).

In the thermal spectrum, orange (40–55°C) maps to the outer surface of warm-blooded mammals at rest — the fur-covered exterior of a jaguar still retaining metabolic heat after a hunt, or the exposed skin zones of a foraging gorilla.

APPETITE

Food Color Signaling

Orange is the most appetite-stimulating color in the spectrum. It signals ripeness, sweetness, and caloric density — fruit at peak nutrition. Fast food chains exploit this biological signal intentionally.

ATTENTION

Warning Without Danger

Safety engineers selected orange for high-visibility equipment because it captures attention with urgency but without the freeze response triggered by red. It compels action without panic.

WILDLIFE

Feline Thermal Pattern

Thermal imaging of big cats like leopards and jaguars shows orange-dominant body temperature signatures. The high surface area-to-mass ratio of these cats maintains 40–55°C surface readings during activity.

PINK / PLASMA

In thermal false-color palettes, pink and magenta often represent the transition zone between high surface heat and deep tissue temperature — approximately 35–45°C. Psychologically, pink's stimulating properties are documented: Baker-Miller Pink was studied by Schauss (1979) for its temporary muscle-weakening effect on agitated individuals.

This pink-to-magenta zone in wildlife thermography appears around the bare facial skin, ears, and mucosal areas of primates — zones where superficial blood flow is highest and skin is thinnest.
