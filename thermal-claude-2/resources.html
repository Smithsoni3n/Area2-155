PRIMARY LITERATURE

ACADEMIC STUDIES

COLOR PSYCHOLOGY

Elliot & Maier (2007)

Landmark study establishing that red exposure causes measurable autonomic nervous system activation — increased heart rate, blood pressure, and heightened alertness — within seconds of exposure. Replicated across 6 experiments.

Journal of Experimental Psychology
→ VIEW SOURCE
COGNITIVE SCIENCE

Mehta & Zhu (2009)

Blue ambient light improved creative performance by 15% compared to red. Red environments enhanced detail-oriented, analytical tasks. The first study to systematically distinguish color effects by cognitive task type.

Science, Vol. 323
→ VIEW SOURCE
EVOLUTIONARY BIOLOGY

Hill & Barton (2005)

Olympic combat sport athletes wearing red won significantly more bouts than those in blue. Proposed mechanism: red signals dominance and aggression in primates, triggering submission responses in opponents.

Nature, Vol. 435
→ VIEW SOURCE
CLINICAL RESEARCH

Schauss (1979)

Baker-Miller Pink (a specific bubblegum-pink shade) measurably reduced aggressive behavior and muscle strength in incarcerated individuals. Effect duration approximately 15–30 minutes. Replicated in correctional facilities.

Orthomolecular Psychiatry
→ VIEW SOURCE
ENVIRONMENTAL DESIGN

Jalil, Yunus & Said (2012)

Orange-colored environments increased self-reported social comfort and reduced task anxiety compared to neutral environments. First study measuring color effects on interaction frequency in educational spaces.

Procedia — Social and Behavioral Sciences
→ VIEW SOURCE
AFFECTIVE SCIENCE

Russell (1980)

The Circumplex Model of Affect maps all emotional states on two axes: valence (positive/negative) and arousal (high/low). Foundational framework for understanding how color maps to emotional state through temperature association.

Journal of Personality and Social Psychology
→ VIEW SOURCE
THERMAL IMAGING

Nakayama et al. (2005)

Long-wave infrared thermal imaging of primate face and body during emotional states showed measurable temperature increases in the nasal dorsum and periorbital regions during stress — a non-invasive stress biomarker.

Physiology & Behavior
→ VIEW SOURCE
WILDLIFE RESEARCH

McCafferty et al. (2011)

Thermal imaging of free-ranging great apes in African jungle habitats established baseline thermal profiles. Demonstrates that thermal cameras can identify individual gorillas and detect health anomalies through heat pattern changes.

PLOS ONE
→ VIEW SOURCE
MEDICAL IMAGING

Ring & Ammer (2012)

Comprehensive review of infrared thermal imaging in clinical medicine. Establishes standard color palettes used in FLIR and similar medical-grade thermal cameras. Baseline reference for all false-color temperature mapping in this project.

Physiological Measurement

SPECTRUM GALLERY

THERMAL COLOR REFERENCE

ZONE 01 — VIOLET / BLACK
−40°C

The thermal floor. Below measurable biological activity. Represents deep space, polar environments, and objects at cryogenic temperatures. In false-color palettes this is often shown as black-violet — the absence of heat.

ZONE 02 — BLUE
−10°C

Cool ambient temperatures. Jungle air, shaded ground, and the extremities of warm-blooded animals in cool environments. Psychologically the most calming hue — lowest cortisol and heart rate response of any visible wavelength.

ZONE 03 — CYAN
10°C

The transition between cool and neutral. Damp soil, humid forest air, and breath-cooled zones around animal muzzles. Hospital environments use cyan-tinted lighting because it reduces patient anxiety scores by measurable margins.

ZONE 04 — GREEN
25°C

Ambient room temperature and the outer surface of warm-blooded animals at rest. Green represents ecological safety at the neurological level — the signal of abundant food, shelter, and absence of threat.

ZONE 05 — YELLOW / AMBER
35–45°C

Warm zones — gorilla outer body at rest, the heated exhale of mammals, exposed primate skin. Yellow-amber activates moderate alertness and social signaling. Most visible wavelength under low-contrast conditions.

ZONE 06 — ORANGE
55°C

Active muscle surface, facial skin of alert primates, the nasal and periorbital zones of stressed animals. Orange is the intersection of danger and opportunity — appetite, urgency, and social engagement all converge.

ZONE 07 — RED / WHITE
70°C+

Peak biological heat emission. The fastest autonomic response of any color. Heart rate +18%, blood pressure elevated, fight-or-flight engaged. In wildlife: a charging great ape, a jaguar mid-sprint, exposed skin under peak muscular exertion.
