Service Dog Facts

Learn about the incredible work service dogs do and how they transform lives every single day.

2+ Years of Training

Service dogs undergo rigorous training for 2-3 years, learning over 90 commands and tasks specific to their handler's disability.

They master public access skills, task work, and maintaining focus in any environment.

AKC Service Dog Training

PTSD Support

Click to learn more

Trained PTSD Tasks

PTSD service dogs perform critical tasks: grounding during flashbacks, creating physical barriers in crowds, interrupting nightmares, and providing deep pressure therapy.

They can detect anxiety before the handler notices.

VA.gov PTSD Service Dogs

Legal Protection

ADA Rights

Under the Americans with Disabilities Act, service dogs are allowed in ALL public spaces, including restaurants, stores, and hospitals.

Businesses can only ask two questions: Is this a service dog? What tasks is it trained to perform?

ADA.gov Official FAQ

Not Pets

Click to learn more

Medical Equipment

Service dogs are working medical equipment, not pets. They provide life-saving assistance and must remain focused on their handler at all times.

Distracting a service dog can be dangerous.

ADA National Network

PTSD is Real

Not all disabilities are visible. PTSD, anxiety disorders, and other psychiatric conditions are just as valid as physical disabilities.

Service dogs for psychiatric conditions perform measurable, trained tasks.

NIMH PTSD Information

Handler's Rights

Click to learn more

Privacy Matters

Handlers are NOT required to disclose their disability, provide documentation, or explain what their dog does beyond the two ADA questions.

Respect their privacy and right to access public spaces.

ADA.gov Service Animals

Did You Know?

Service dogs can detect changes in heart rate, blood pressure, and cortisol levels through scent. They often alert to panic attacks or flashbacks before the handler consciously realizes what's happening.

This early intervention can prevent full-blown episodes and help handlers use coping strategies before symptoms escalate.
