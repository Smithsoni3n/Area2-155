SHIFT

Early 1960s: PSNS Dungarees

Puget Sound Naval Shipyard remained one of Bremerton's largest employers, with tradesmen reporting in denim dungarees and utility shirts for maintenance-heavy shifts.

Early 1960s: Clinical Whites

Regional clinics and naval hospital staff wore heavily starched white uniforms, with caps and polished shoes reflecting strict cleanliness standards.

Late 1960s: Service Station Shift

As car ownership surged around Kitsap County, station attendants wore logo shirts, caps, and durable cotton trousers for full-service fueling culture.

Late 1960s: Retail & Stewardess Precision

Professional women in retail and aviation-adjacent services often wore coordinated tailoring, gloves, and practical heels to signal polished reliability.

1970s: Maritime & Logging Toughwear

Heavy wool layers, waxed jackets, and work boots defined Pacific Northwest fieldwear as maritime and timber labor demanded weatherproof durability.

1970s: Women in Shipyard Coveralls

By the 1970s, more women entered industrial roles, adopting steel-toe boots and coveralls suited for welding, fabrication, and dockside mechanics.
